    Slide 03Why a segmentation model alone does not fix it

    A model always returns something

    That is the whole problem. Good open segmentation models exist; the missing piece is knowing which of their thousand outputs to trust.

    Without a trustworthy confidence signal, a designer still has to open all 1,000 files to find the 50 bad ones — and the bottleneck has not moved at all. Worse: one bad mask on a live product page costs more than the review it skipped.

    Slide 06 shows the specific mask that makes this concrete: sharp, clean, perfectly anti-aliased — and cut around completely the wrong object. Every edge-quality metric is happy with it.

    So the deliverable is not a mask. It is a mask plus an auditable decision about that mask.

    Slide 04What we built

    One microservice, eight stages, four ways in

    Deliberate choices

    • BiRefNet (MIT) as the primary matte — state of the art on high-resolution boundaries, and licensed for commercial use.
    • Two-pass ROI refinement: the second pass runs on a crop around the product, so a garment filling 40% of a 4000 px frame gets 2.5× the sampling density on its own edge.
    • Three-deep backend registry — BiRefNet → U2-Net → GrabCut. A missing weight file degrades the service instead of failing the request.

    Hard guarantees

    • The mask is never resized. EXIF orientation is applied once, on load, and those pixel dimensions become the contract for every artifact.
    • A bad URL or corrupt file is a result row, not an exception — one dead CDN link cannot kill a 500-image batch.
    • Every QC term ships in the response, so a verdict is auditable rather than a number.

    Slide 07How the AI judges itself

    Nine no-reference signals, then five hard vetoes

    No ground truth exists at inference time, so every signal is computed from the image and the mask alone. Bar length is the signal's weight in the confidence score.

    edge alignment .20 Does the outline sit on a real gradient? Measured on the Sobel field against the image's own median gradient — scale-free, so it works on a white-on-white sweep where a fixed Canny pair reports "no edges".
    ensemble agreement .20 Does an architecturally unrelated model agree, on region and on edge placement? The one signal that does not share the primary model's blind spots.
    boundary contrast .14 Is there a colour step across the outline, relative to the local noise floor? Catches a mask that has absorbed a neighbouring object.
    ramp sharpness .12 Uncertain pixels ÷ boundary length. 1–3 px is correct anti-aliasing; over 12 px composites as a grey halo.
    background consistency .10 Do the pixels just outside the mask look like the backdrop? The only signal that catches a sharp, well-aligned cut of the wrong thing.
    topology .10 Fragmentation, solidity, and hole count against the category's expectation — zero holes on a mug is as suspicious as forty on a canvas.
    coverage .07 Plausible product-to-frame ratio for the category. Catches "matted the whole backdrop" and "found nothing".
    border contact .05 A product running off the frame cannot be a clean base.
    shape prior .02 Class-conditional silhouette sanity, with thresholds calibrated from measured data rather than guessed.
    Then the vetoes. However good the aggregate score, a mask never reads READY if it is fragmented, clipped by the frame, apparently a sub-region of a larger object, atypically shaped for its class, cutting through an object, or if the cross-check model materially disagrees.

    Not a pass-through wrapper. The quality decision is computed here, from nine independent signals, one of which is a second model we run ourselves. Swap the segmenter for a third-party API and this layer is unchanged.

    Slide 09The cases the brief names

    Hair, folds, shadows, handles

    1. Hair across a garment. A saliency model returns the smooth garment outline including the pixels the hair covers, so the mockup paints the design straight over the hair. A morphological black-hat finds thin dark structures inside the mask body, then six guards decide what to remove: interior only, clearly darker than the local garment tone, thin, long and stringy per component, within an 8% budget, and lying on a locally thick part of the product. Worth +0.07 boundary F1 on apparel.
    2. Fabric folds. Kept, not smoothed — the fold shading becomes the displacement and shadow maps instead of contaminating the mask.
    3. Cast shadow. A studio drop shadow has a signature: low alpha, desaturated, darker than the backdrop but nowhere near as dark as a real product edge. Detected and removed, so artwork never lands on a shadow.
    4. Mug and tote handles. Structural holes are kept and counted, and the count feeds the quality score.
    5. Busy backgrounds. Where the pipeline cannot fix the ambiguity, cross-check disagreement flags it. Detection without correction is still worth shipping — it is the difference between a review queue and a lottery.
    The guard list above is not theoretical. Running the pipeline over real Creative-Commons product photos caught the first version of the hair remover deleting the word "AIRTOUCH" off a tote bag and most of a rune ring off another — printed lettering is also thin, dark and inside the mask. The component-geometry guards fixed it: 1,055 components rejected on that image, one kept, and the synthetic hair case unchanged.
    Slide 10Performance

    2.19 seconds a mask on a 4 GB laptop GPU

    Deliberately the weakest plausible deployment target. A T4 or L4 is roughly two to three times faster.

    Scaling is sub-linear on purpose: segmentation is serialised by a per-backend lock, because two CUDA graphs must not be in flight at once on a 4 GB card. What the worker pool buys is overlapping the CPU half of the pipeline — decode, refine, QC, PNG encode, a combined 54% of the budget — with GPU inference.

    What each knob is actually worth — measured, not assumed
    SettingMeasured effect
    SHADOW_MAPS=0, DISPLACEMENT=0 −1.9 s (−47%) — the biggest lever, and free if the mockup engine only needs the mask. Almost all of it is PNG encoding, not compute.
    INFER_SIZE=1280 +3.1 s for no measurable accuracy gain. Not worth it.
    INFER_SIZE=768 −95 ms only — the second ROI pass grows as the first shrinks.
    ENSEMBLE=0 −449 ms, but removes the signal that guarantees no false accepts. Do not, if you act on READY automatically.
    BIREFNET_REPO=…/BiRefNet_lite ~3× faster, visibly softer on hair. The right call for CPU-only hosts.
    Slide 11Integration

    A microservice, not a notebook

    Four ways in

    • POST /v1/mask — one or many uploaded files
    • POST /v1/mask/url — a direct image URL: the service-to-service path
    • POST /v1/mask/manifest — CSV or JSON from the PIM, with tolerant column matching
    • scripts/run_folder.py — a drop folder, for air-gapped runs

    And out

    • Swagger at /docs, review dashboard at /
    • Batch report with automation KPIs, review queue first, and original-vs-mask overlays
    • Optional X-API-Key; the artifact store is a one-file swap for S3

    The print area drops straight in

    Returned as a quad plus the 3×3 homography mapping a unit design canvas onto it — exactly what libvips, ImageMagick or a canvas engine needs. No mockup-engine changes.

    1. warp design onto print_area.quad   ← homography
2. displace with displacement_map     ← folds
3. multiply by shadow_map             ← creases
4. screen with highlight_map          ← sheen
5. mask with alpha_mask               ← containment

    Verified by test, not by eye: the print-area quad contains zero pixels outside the product mask, and its homography maps the unit square onto the returned corners to within 1e-3.

    Slide 13Limits we are not hiding

    Where this is honestly weak

    1. Semantic ambiguity is unsolvable from pixels. Nothing in a photo says whether the frame around a print is part of the product. We detect the ambiguity and ask. The real fix is the category arriving from the PIM, or a fine-tune on the customer's library.
    2. Adjacent touching objects can be absorbed into the mask. Detected via cross-check disagreement and the shape prior — not yet corrected.
    3. Rendered ground truth is an upper bound on realism. The real-photo spot check is qualitative. Validate against ~50 hand-labelled real bases before committing to an SLA.
    4. The QC weights are hand-set. Calibrated against measured data rather than intuition, but a learned scorer over the same nine features would place the threshold optimally instead of conservatively.
    5. n = 19. Enough to support "zero false accepts" and ρ = 0.81 as directional evidence; not enough for tight confidence intervals.
    Slide 14Roadmap for the final round

    Five things, in the order they pay off

    1. Fine-tune BiRefNet on the customer's own base library. A few hundred labelled in-domain bases should close most of the remaining gap, especially the frame-versus-print ambiguity.
    2. Learn the QC scorer from real review decisions — same nine features, gradient-boosted. Turns a conservative threshold into an optimal one, which lifts the automation rate directly.
    3. TensorRT / ONNX export for 2–3× on the same GPU. Note from the stage split that this removes ~500 ms of 2,190: past that the pipeline is CPU-bound, so pair it with more batch workers.
    4. Auto-correct adjacent-object absorption by using the cross-check mask as a region prior, not only as a QC signal.
    5. Photometric-stereo displacement if the studio can shoot two flash positions — turns today's single-image estimate into a measurement.
    Closing line. The mask is table stakes. What we are proposing is a base pipeline that knows when to hand work back to a human — and on our numbers, it handed back every single one of the bad ones.
